# Idea Brief Template

Use this template when creating `idea_brief.md` artifacts.

## Template

```markdown
# Idea Brief: [Working Title]

## One-Line Pitch
[Single sentence: "[Product] helps [customer] [achieve outcome] by [method]."]

## Problem Statement
[2-3 sentences describing:]
- The specific pain point
- Why it matters (frequency, severity, cost)
- Why current solutions fall short

## Target Customer
[Initial hypothesis about who has this problem:]
- Primary segment description
- Key characteristics (demographic/firmographic)
- Behavioral indicators ("People who...")

## Solution Hypothesis
[How your product addresses the problem:]
- Core approach/mechanism
- Key capabilities (3-5 bullet points)
- Why this approach might work

## Key Assumptions
[3-5 assumptions that must be validated:]
1. [Customer assumption] - e.g., "Users currently spend X hours/week on this task"
2. [Problem assumption] - e.g., "Existing solutions are too expensive for SMBs"
3. [Solution assumption] - e.g., "Automation can reduce time by 80%"
4. [Market assumption] - e.g., "Users will pay $X/month for this"
5. [Execution assumption] - e.g., "This can be built with available APIs"

## Success Metrics

### Validation Phase
- [Metric 1]: [target] (e.g., "50 customer interviews completed")
- [Metric 2]: [target] (e.g., "10 letters of intent collected")

### Launch Phase
- [Metric 1]: [target] (e.g., "1,000 users in first month")
- [Metric 2]: [target] (e.g., "$10K MRR by month 3")

## Created
[YYYY-MM-DD]

## Status
Draft
```

## Field Guidance

### Working Title
- Keep it short (3-7 words)
- Make it memorable and descriptive
- Can be a product name or descriptive phrase
- Examples: "AI Home Energy Auditor", "SMB Compliance Automation"

### One-Line Pitch
- Maximum 150 characters
- Follow the format: "[Product] helps [customer] [achieve outcome] by [method]"
- Be specific, not generic
- Bad: "A platform for businesses"
- Good: "Automates SOC 2 compliance for seed-stage startups"

### Problem Statement
- Focus on the problem, not the solution
- Include evidence of severity if available
- Mention what's broken about alternatives
- Be concrete, not abstract

### Target Customer
- Be specific enough to find these people
- Include "negative" criteria (who it's NOT for)
- Think about who has the problem most acutely
- Consider who controls budget/decision

### Solution Hypothesis
- Focus on the "how" not the "what"
- Explain the insight or approach
- Keep feature list high-level
- Tie back to the problem

### Key Assumptions
- Make them testable/falsifiable
- Order by risk (most critical first)
- Include assumptions across categories:
  - Customer: Do they have this problem?
  - Problem: Is it severe enough?
  - Solution: Will this solve it?
  - Market: Will they pay?
  - Execution: Can we build it?

### Success Metrics
- Make them measurable
- Set realistic targets
- Split by phase (validation vs launch)
- Include leading indicators

### Status Values
- **Draft**: Initial creation, needs review
- **In Validation**: Actively being validated
- **Validated**: Key assumptions confirmed
- **Pivoted**: Significant change based on learnings
- **Abandoned**: Idea not worth pursuing
